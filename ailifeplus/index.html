AILiFE+: An Inclusive AI Literacy and Workforce Readiness Framework for Disabled People in Ireland

Understanding how AI literacy can be made more accessible and useful for disabled people in Ireland

Project image or illustration
About the project

What do disabled people in Ireland think of AI — and how can AI literacy work for them?

Artificial intelligence is reshaping work, education, and public life. For disabled people, these changes carry both significant opportunities and serious risks. AILiFE+ asks: how can AI literacy be made genuinely useful, accessible, and rights-affirming?

This project brings together researchers from UCD's School of Information and Communication Studies and community members from the Independent Living Movement Ireland (ILMI) to explore these questions through a participatory, rights-based approach.

By placing disabled people at the centre of the research — not as subjects, but as co-investigators and knowledge-holders — AILiFE+ aims to develop AI literacy frameworks and resources that reflect lived experience and promote workforce readiness on equitable terms.

Our work spans surveys, focus groups, co-design workshops, and public outputs that are accessible, open, and community-led.

Our Activities

Project image

Understanding the Landscape: Community Survey

We are gathering perspectives from disabled people across Ireland on their experiences with AI tools, their confidence with new technologies, and the barriers they face to meaningful participation.

Focus group image

Diving Deep: Participatory Focus Groups

Working alongside ILMI community champions, we are running peer-led focus groups that allow participants to share their experiences with AI more deeply — moving beyond surveys to stories, strategies, and priorities.

Output image

Sharing Knowledge: Resources and Co-Design

Using findings from the survey and focus groups, we will co-produce accessible AI literacy resources, policy briefs, and practical tools — designed with and for disabled communities.
